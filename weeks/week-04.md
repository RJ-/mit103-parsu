# Week 04 — Information Gathering: Interactive Methods

**Program outcomes:** `b.4` · `f.3`  
**Portfolio thread:** System Proposal — elicitation instruments

## Learning outcomes

By the end of this week you should be able to:

- Recognize the value of interactive methods for information gathering.
- Construct interview questions that elicit human information requirements.
- Structure interviews in a way that is meaningful to users.
- Explain the concept of JAD and judge when to use it.
- Write effective questions to survey users about their work, and design and administer effective questionnaires.

## In class

- **Lecture discussion (30 min).** Interview structures: pyramid, funnel, diamond. Open vs closed questions and what each buys you.
- **Interactive learning — interview role-play (60 min).** Triads rotate: analyst, interviewee, observer. The observer scores against the interview rubric and gives feedback immediately. Rotate twice so everyone runs an interview.
- **JAD walkthrough (30 min).** Structure a hypothetical JAD session for one student's domain: who attends, who facilitates, what artefact leaves the room.

## Lab and independent work

- Draft an interview guide (8–12 questions) using the [interview guide template](../templates/interview-guide.md).
- Draft a questionnaire for a broader user group. Justify every closed-question scale you choose.
- Peer-review one classmate's instrument through a pull request comment.

## Assessment

System Proposal (Laboratory/Performance, 40%). Instruments are assessed on construct quality, not length.

## Check yourself

- Which of your questions leads the respondent toward the answer you already expect? Rewrite it.
- You can interview six people or survey sixty. For your domain, which yields more defensible requirements, and why?
